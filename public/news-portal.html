AI Regulation Bill Passes Congress with Bipartisan Majority

Landmark legislation establishes first federal framework for autonomous agent deployment, requiring cognitive verification protocols for systems operating above 1015 FLOPS.

By Sarah Chen 2 hours ago

WASHINGTON — The Artificial Intelligence Accountability Act passed the Senate 78-22 Thursday, marking the most significant federal intervention into AI development since the technology's commercial emergence. The bill mandates cognitive toll verification — a timing-based humanity proof — for any autonomous system exceeding the computational threshold, effectively creating a "speed limit" for machine intelligence.

"We're not regulating what AI can think," said Sen. Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.), chair of the Commerce Committee. "We're ensuring that when something thinks at machine speed, we know it's a machine." The legislation directly responds to the proliferation of local LLM deployments — models like Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.6 running on consumer hardware — which have enabled coordinated agent swarms capable of bypassing traditional CAPTCHA and rate-limiting defenses.

The bill now heads to the president's desk, where a signature is expected within days. Industry groups have already signaled legal challenges, arguing the 1,500-millisecond cognitive threshold arbitrarily discriminates against optimized inference pipelines.
